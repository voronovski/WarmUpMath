About Warm Up Math

A lightweight, installable app for practicing elementary school arithmetic — addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. No accounts, no ads; it works offline once installed, and all progress stays on your device.

Modes

Session
A timed quiz. Choose a rubric, a difficulty, how many questions, and how many seconds you get per question, then hit Start. Answer each problem before the timer runs out — at the end you get a score, the total time, and a per-question breakdown.
Marathon
Choose a difficulty and hit Start — the app runs a 15-question set for each rubric in turn (Addition, Multiplication, Subtraction, Division, 60 questions total), each timed using the same seconds-per-question limit set on the Session tab. It doesn't stop on a wrong answer — you go through all 60, then get a letter grade for each rubric plus an overall grade for the run.
Generate
Build your own worksheet. Add one or more formula sets (e.g. a + b, a * b - c) with how many questions you want from each, tap Generate to build the worksheet, fill in the answers, then Check to see what you got right.

Progress tracking

The Session tab shows an error-rate history chart and the Marathon tab shows a grade history chart; the home screen also lists your recent attempts. Everything is stored locally in the browser (localStorage) — nothing is sent anywhere.

Privacy & offline use

  • No personal data is collected.
  • All your history (error rates, marathon grades, recent attempts) is stored only on your device, in the browser's local storage.
  • Once installed, the app works fully offline — no internet connection needed after the first visit.

Install as an app

The app can be installed to a phone's home screen or a desktop and used offline after that.

  • Android / Chrome: the browser shows an "Install" prompt automatically, or use the menu → "Install app".
  • iOS / Safari: tap Share → "Add to Home Screen".
  • Desktop (Chrome/Edge): click the install icon in the address bar.

Note: installation and offline caching require the site to be served over HTTPS (or localhost) — it won't work over plain HTTP.
